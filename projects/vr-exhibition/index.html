THE EXPERIENCE

The Computer Science display was not one demo. It was a room that needed enough things to see, try, and understand—and people to keep the whole experience running.

01 / Building the room

For the school’s 50th-anniversary exhibition, I contributed to the Computer Science room. The materials I remember making include a range of VR worlds for Meta Quest, VR videos, animations, presentations, and demonstrations.

I also helped with other subject displays, but the Computer Science room needed me throughout the exhibition. The work stretched from making the content to getting things set up and ready for visitors.

02 / The long day

We spent long hours preparing and setting up. I arrived early and stayed focused on the Computer Science room, so I did not get much chance to see the other models I had contributed to. The exhibition was a good reminder that the final public moment depends on a lot of work visitors never see.

03 / What I learned

A room full of technology still needs a clear experience. People need to know where to look, what to try, and how to move from one demo to another. Making the exhibits taught me to think about sequencing and presentation as well as the content itself.
