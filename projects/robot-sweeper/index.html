PROJECT NOTEBOOK / 03

Robotic
sweeper

A remote-controlled car adapted with extra motors to try out a simple cleaning idea.

Early experiment / not autonomous
ElectronicsMotors · repurposingEarly school years
THE QUESTION

Could a toy car be made to do a little more than move from one side of the room to the other?

01 / Starting with what I had

I took apart a remote-controlled car and adapted it for a small robotic-vacuum idea. I added downward-facing motors that could act as sweepers, turning a familiar toy into something with a different job.

It was one of my earliest experiments with modifying hardware. I was interested in how each motor could change what the vehicle did, and how to make the idea more interesting with the parts I could find.

02 / The first version

This was a remote-controlled prototype experiment, not an autonomous cleaner. I had not yet learned enough programming to build navigation or sensing into it. Later, the PIR sensor and Arduino work became separate experiments as I began discovering electronics and code.

03 / What it taught me

A project does not need to be complicated to teach something. This one made me think about how a motor changes a mechanism, what a reused platform makes possible, and how far an idea can go before it needs better control and more careful design.

There is no surviving photograph of this build in the supplied archive, so this case study uses a restrained diagram instead of implying a photograph exists.

04 / What I would revisit

If I returned to the idea, I would begin by defining what “cleaning” means for a small test surface, then measure how much the sweeper collects. I would keep sensing, movement, and cleaning as separate questions before trying to combine them.
