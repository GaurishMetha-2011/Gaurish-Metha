PROJECT NOTEBOOK / 01

AETHER

A transparent solar-window concept exploring whether part of sunlight that ordinary glass lets through could be redirected toward photovoltaic cells.

Concept / research and presentation
Renewable energyMaterials · optics · photovoltaicsMeasurement not documented
THE QUESTION

Can a window remain useful as a window and also make use of some light that would otherwise pass through it?

01 / The idea

AETHER began as a student concept for energy-generating architectural glass. Its earlier name was AURS, used when the concept was first presented at a Middle East College sustainability and innovation hackathon. I later renamed it AETHER and developed the idea for the Indian School Muscat Techathon and SIC Conclave.

The name refers to an old idea of an unseen medium associated with the upper sky. The project phrase “Invisible Light, Visible Impact” reflects the interest in light people cannot see.

02 / Proposed architecture

The research direction explores a transparent or translucent luminescent layer that absorbs ultraviolet light, re-emits light at another wavelength, and guides some of it toward photovoltaic cells along the window edge.

Carbon quantum dots came into my research as a possible luminescent material, including the idea of exploring carbon-rich agricultural waste as a source. This remains a proposed material direction: the composition, processing method, optical behaviour, lifetime, safety, and suitability for an actual window have not been established here.

Concept, not result. The diagram and descriptions explain a hypothesis. They are not measurements of power, efficiency, transmittance, lifetime, or durability.

03 / From pitch to prototype

At the Middle East College event, my teammate and I presented the early AURS version. We adapted it into a second presentation for the school Techathon. AETHER later received third place at the SIC Conclave.

After early competition results, I began looking more closely at the material question and the requirements for a functional optical-to-electrical demonstration. I have not documented a validated working window or measured output on this site.

A possible next experiment would need to isolate one claim at a time: optical absorption and emission first, then light guiding, then whether a small edge-mounted photovoltaic cell produces measurable electrical output under controlled conditions. Each step would need recorded conditions and repeatable measurements.

04 / What remains open

Materials: Can a proposed luminescent material be made consistently, safely, and in a way that remains optically useful?

Optics: How much light is absorbed, re-emitted, trapped, and lost? How does that change visible clarity?

Electrical output: What, if anything, can the edge cells produce in a repeatable test, and how should the output be measured?

Durability: How does the material respond to heat, moisture, oxygen, and prolonged light exposure?

Until experiments answer those questions, AETHER remains a concept with competition presentations, not a validated product or a claim that building windows can yet supply useful power.

05 / Evidence and context

AETHER received third place at the SIC Conclave at Indian School Muscat. At the EurekaDev Hackathon, it also received third place in the Research track; that recognition is documented independently in the Achievements archive.

Muscat Daily published an article about the solar-window idea. News coverage provides context, not experimental validation.
