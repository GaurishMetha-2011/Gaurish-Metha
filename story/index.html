MECHANIX sets / COVID period / India

COVID PERIOD / INDIA

My first builds were mechanical.

During COVID, while we were living in India, my parents bought me several MECHANIX construction sets. They came with metal strips, rods, screws, nuts, and other parts that could be assembled into different structures and models.

I spent a lot of time building with them. This was my first real interest in making things, before electronics and Arduino.

Motors.
Circuits. Moving models.

Electronics kit / school STEM builds

LATER / ELECTRONICS AND SCHOOL BUILDS

Then I started exploring motors and circuits.

Later in Oman, my father bought me an electronics kit. Motors and circuits caught my attention, and I began to work out how parts made things move.

I also made physical models for school STEM exhibitions, including a hydraulic bridge and a Ferris wheel. They were early school builds that let me make an idea, show how I thought it worked, and explain it to other people.

Mistake the part.

A sweeper idea / a sensor that looked like a disco light

EARLY EXPERIMENTS / PIR AND ARDUINO

A disco-light sensor led me to Arduino.

I wanted to make a robot vacuum, so I adapted a remote-controlled car with downward-facing motors to act as sweepers. It was an early hands-on experiment, not an autonomous cleaner.

Later, I ordered a sensor because I thought it looked like a disco light. It turned out to be a PIR sensor. I looked up how to use it, found Arduino, and started testing sensors one by one. I eventually ordered a set of roughly 27 sensors, many of them new to me.

A short Arduino course gave me more structure, but I was still learning by searching, taking things apart, and trying small experiments.

GENERATIVE AI / LOCAL MODELS

A new tool, then ten new questions.

My interests shifted toward generative AI around the end of seventh standard. I started with tools such as ChatGPT, tried prompt writing, and followed the possibilities into AI filmmaking and image generation.

At a free AI course, one passing mention stayed with me: you could run models locally. I went looking for what that meant, tried Ollama and ComfyUI on my laptop, and learned that what a computer can do depends a lot on the hardware underneath it. I couldn’t run everything I wanted, but learning why was part of the appeal.

I also explored automation with Make and n8n, and connected tools through MCP. One early AI-assisted experiment gave me analysis I couldn’t trust. It was a useful reminder: connecting tools can make a workflow look impressive without making its answers correct. I still use AI-assisted coding while I build my programming skills; it helps me explore an idea, but I want to understand what the code is doing.

Hardware / operating systems / software

COMPUTING / HARDWARE AND SOFTWARE

I wanted to know what was under the case.

My first laptop opened up questions about CPUs, graphics cards, memory, motherboards, storage, and why some computers can run software or models that others cannot. I became curious about operating systems and the software running on top of the hardware, too.

I compared PC parts and configurations and looked into single-board computers such as Raspberry Pi. That was research and comparison, not building a custom PC.

SERVERS / NETWORKS / SMART HOME

The fun was in seeing how the pieces connect.

Learning about home servers led me to explore NAS, networking, Wi-Fi access points, virtual machines, and systems such as TrueNAS and Proxmox. I also looked into smart-home standards including Zigbee, Matter, Thread, Wi-Fi, and Z-Wave, and tried Home Assistant on an older computer.

Much of this has been research, diagrams, and limited experiments, not a professional server build or a complete smart-home installation. I became more interested in how a system could work together than in simply choosing one device.

CREATIVE TECHNOLOGY / SCHOOL CONTRIBUTIONS

Technology became a way to build an experience.

For my school’s 50th-anniversary exhibition, I contributed to several subject displays. In the Computer Science room, I helped prepare Meta Quest VR experiences, made virtual worlds and animations, recorded VR clips, and put together presentations and demonstrations.

I also edited school event teasers and contributed to other displays, including a hydraulic model for STEM. These were creative and school contributions, not separate software products or commercial projects.

Preparing for the exhibition meant setup, explaining, troubleshooting, and helping the room work for its visitors as well as making the content.

LARGER IDEAS / CLASSECHO AND AETHER

Questions became projects to develop.

At a school AI Thon in Class 9, I developed ClassEcho, an idea for turning lessons into summaries and personalised revision material. It was a project concept, not a deployed classroom product.

A separate idea about transparent solar windows began as AURS. I later developed it as AETHER, researching luminescent materials and ways to guide light toward photovoltaic cells at the window edge. The concept has not produced a documented working window or measured output.

ClassEcho and AETHER are different projects, each with its own questions and limits. Their case studies explain what I explored and what remains untested.

CURRENT DIRECTION / ENGINEERING

Hardware and software. Both sides of the question.

I’m looking toward Electronics and Communication Engineering. I’m especially interested in electronics, embedded systems, and semiconductor technology, where physical components and software meet.

I want to strengthen the fundamentals, design more complete hardware, and test what I build. One current design exercise is a KiCad PCB for an obstacle-avoidance car, arranged to give its components room on the board. Longer term, I hope to work at ASML in the Netherlands.
